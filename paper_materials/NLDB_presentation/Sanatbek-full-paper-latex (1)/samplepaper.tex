\begin{abstract}
Efficient low-resource dependency parsing requires extracting maximal
syntactic signal from limited supervision with minimal additional
machinery. We introduce \textsc{UzUDT}, a newly created gold-standard Universal Dependencies treebank for Uzbek, manually annotated in
\textsc{INCEpTION} by six annotators with full adjudication
(684~sentences, \mbox{$\sim$7.8k}~tokens); \textsc{UzUDT} is
currently the largest publicly available Uzbek UD treebank.
We then study two complementary robustness strategies that remain
lightweight in practice:
(i)~\emph{linguistically-motivated last-subword fusion}, a positional
heuristic for aggregating BERT WordPieces into UD token
representations, and
(ii)~\emph{cross-treebank training} by merging genre-complementary
Uzbek UD resources.
Using a Stanza-based BiLSTM tagger and biaffine parser augmented with monolingual contextual embeddings (TahrirchiBERT), we conduct a
controlled factorial comparison across three embedding\slash fusion
configurations and two data settings. Cross-treebank training yields the largest gains, improving LAS by 9.62--11.16~points while nearly doubling the training set (451~$\rightarrow$~781~sentences).
The best configuration (\textit{TahrirchiBERT} with last-subword
fusion on merged data) achieves 85.08~UPOS, 72.39~UAS, and
63.81~LAS.
\keywords{Uzbek \and Universal Dependencies \and Low-resource NLP
\and Dependency Parsing \and Cross-treebank Training \and Subword
Fusion}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Dependency parsing is a central task in NLP, providing structured syntactic representations that support downstream applications such as information extraction, machine translation, and question answering.
The Universal Dependencies (UD) framework has enabled cross-linguistic
consistency in syntactic annotation and facilitated multilingual
research~\cite{nivre-etal-2016-universal}.
Modern neural parsers, particularly graph-based models with biaffine
attention~\cite{DBLP:conf/iclr/DozatM17}, achieve strong performance
when sufficient annotated data are available and are widely adopted in
toolkits such as Stanza~\cite{qi-etal-2020-stanza}.
However, their success remains heavily dependent on training data size
and quality.

For low-resource and morphologically rich languages, data scarcity
becomes the primary bottleneck.
Uzbek, an agglutinative Turkic language, exhibits rich suffixation,
flexible word order, and significant genre variation, all of which
increase the complexity of morphosyntactic tagging and dependency
parsing.
Despite the existence of Uzbek UD resources within the UD
ecosystem~\cite{de-marneffe-etal-2021-universal}, available treebanks
remain relatively small, limiting robust neural modelling.
To address this gap, we introduce
\textsc{UzUDT},\footnote{\url{https://github.com/UniversalDependencies/UD_Uzbek-UzUDT/tree/dev}}
a newly created gold-standard Uzbek UD treebank (684~sentences,
${\sim}$7.8k~tokens), manually annotated in
\textsc{INCEpTION}~\cite{klie-etal-2018-inception} by six annotators
with full adjudication.
To the best of our knowledge, \textsc{UzUDT} is currently the largest
publicly available Uzbek UD treebank.

Beyond resource creation, this work is positioned within the broader
agenda of \emph{efficient low-resource dependency parsing}.
Rather than relying on heavy architectural modifications, we
investigate two lightweight strategies that can be applied with
minimal additional parameters.
First, we propose a \emph{linguistically-motivated last-subword
fusion} strategy for aligning contextualised WordPiece
representations to UD tokens when using BERT-style
encoders~\cite{devlin-etal-2019-bert}.
Subword-to-token alignment is non-trivial for agglutinative
languages, where suffix chains frequently encode syntactic and
morphological information.
Our approach adopts a positional heuristic selecting the final
subword representation for each token motivated by the fact that
grammatical features in Uzbek are predominantly realised in suffixes.
This method is computationally cheap and avoids introducing additional
attention or pooling layers.

Second, we explore \emph{cross-treebank training} as a data-centric
robustness strategy.
Multi-treebank and multilingual training have previously shown
that expanding structural coverage can substantially improve parsing
performance~\cite{1602.01925,zeman-etal-2018-conll}.
In the context of Uzbek, we merge genre-complementary UD resources to
nearly double the training data size and broaden syntactic coverage.
We systematically evaluate this strategy in combination with two
representation choices: static FastText
embeddings~\cite{bojanowski-etal-2017-enriching} and a monolingual
contextual encoder
(\textit{TahrirchiBERT}~\cite{Mamasaidov2023TahrirchiBERT}).

Our contributions are fourfold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We release \textsc{UzUDT}, the largest Uzbek UD treebank to
      date, strengthening the empirical foundation for Uzbek syntactic
      research.
    \item We train and evaluate neural baselines for Uzbek
      morphosyntactic tagging (UPOS, XPOS, UFeats) and dependency
      parsing (UAS, LAS) under a standard UD evaluation protocol.
    \item We introduce a linguistically-motivated last-subword fusion
      heuristic for efficient contextual token alignment in
      agglutinative settings.
    \item Through a controlled factorial comparison across three
      embedding\slash fusion configurations and two data settings, we
      demonstrate that cross-treebank training provides the largest
      LAS gains, while contextual embeddings and last-subword fusion
      offer complementary improvements, particularly for tagging and
      parsing respectively.
\end{enumerate}
\section{Related Work}

\noindent\textit{Universal Dependencies.}
Universal Dependencies (UD) provides a cross-linguistically consistent framework in which words are annotated as syntactic units with morphological properties and grammatical relations. This representation facilitates multilingual evaluation, typological comparison, and cross-lingual transfer. \cite{de-marneffe-etal-2021-universal} review the application of UD to more than one hundred languages and highlight the role of universal POS tags and morphological features in enabling comparative research. The UD guidelines (Version~2\footnote{\url{https://universaldependencies.org/guidelines.html}}) further specify principles for tokenization, multiword expressions, lemmas, UPOS/XPOS tags, morphological features, and dependency relations.\footnote{The author also contributed Uzbek-specific morphological feature and POS documentation to the UD project, e.g., \url{https://universaldependencies.org/uz/feat/PronType.html}.}

\noindent\textit{Parsing in Turkic and other agglutinative languages.}
Dependency parsing in agglutinative languages such as Turkish, Uzbek, and other Turkic languages is challenging due to rich morphology, long suffix chains, flexible word order, and multifunctional grammatical markers \cite{MURZINTCEV2026100195}. Prior work on Turkish has shown that morphology-aware representations improve parsing accuracy; for example, \cite{eryigit-etal-2008-dependency} incorporate morphological segmentation and report gains over lexical baselines. Similar observations have been reported for Kazakh, Uyghur, and Tatar UD treebanks, where parsers benefit from subword-level features and language-specific heuristics for case-driven ambiguities \cite{Marsan_undated-hp}. In addition, \cite{noauthor_2018-qq} discuss the challenges of adapting UD guidelines to Turkish multiword expressions and clitic constructions. These findings motivate our use of neural encoders combined with explicit morphological information.

\noindent\textit{Uzbek resources.}
Uzbek remains under-resourced for UD-style morphosyntactic analysis and dependency parsing, limiting systematic benchmarking and robust parser development. At present, two Uzbek UD treebanks are available. The first, Uzbek-UT, was released in 2023 and contains about 500 sentences \cite{akhundjanova-talamo-2025-universal}. In this study, we build on the newly introduced \emph{UzUDT} (684 sentences, ${\sim}$7.8k tokens), whose design rationale, annotation methodology, and quality assurance are described in a companion paper by the present author \cite{matlatipov2025goldudt}. In the present paper, UzUDT serves as the primary benchmark for evaluating robust neural modeling, morphology-aware preprocessing, and reproducible comparison.

\noindent\textit{Neural pipelines and contextual models.}
Recent years have seen strong progress in fully neural multilingual NLP pipelines. Stanza \cite{Qi2020-zr} introduced a language-agnostic neural pipeline covering tokenization, multiword token expansion, lemmatization, POS and morphological tagging, dependency parsing, and named entity recognition. Its parser builds on the biaffine graph-based architecture of \cite{DBLP:conf/iclr/DozatM17}, which remains a strong baseline for CoNLL-U parsing. \cite{kondratyuk-straka-2019-75} further showed that a single multilingual model (UDify) can parse 75 languages through joint multitask learning. For Uzbek, \cite{kuriyozov-etal-2024-bertbek} introduced \textit{BERTbek}, a transformer model pretrained on a large Uzbek corpus, and showed that monolingual contextual embeddings outperform mBERT on tasks such as text classification and NER.

\noindent\textit{Morphological analysis and hybrid architectures.}
Rule-based morphological analysers remain valuable for agglutinative languages, where they can complement neural models. Apertium is a free and open-source rule-based machine translation platform \cite{10.1007/978-3-031-05328-3_6} with finite-state morphological analysers for many languages \cite{Forcada2011}, including Uzbek \cite{Salaev2024,2301.12711,MURZINTCEV2026100195}. It provides lexicons and morphophonological rules that support lemmatization and feature extraction. For Uzbek, preliminary evidence suggests that combining Apertium-derived features with BERTbek representations improves tagging and parsing robustness, especially for rare and unseen word forms \cite{kuriyozov-etal-2024-bertbek}.
\section{Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}
We use two Uzbek Universal Dependencies treebanks.

\textit{UD\_Uzbek-UzUDT (UzUDT).}
A newly created resource of 684~sentences (${\approx}$7{,}800 tokens) from Uzbek \emph{fiction} (the story \textit{Maqar}, the book \textit{Kun shundan boshlanadi}, and publicly available fairy tales\footnote{\url{https://ertak.uz/}}) and \emph{academic/educational} writing.
Sentences are in the officially standardized Uzbek Latin script, with an average length of ${\approx}$12 tokens.
All annotation---UPOS, XPOS, lemma, morphological features, and dependency relations---follows UD~v2 guidelines~\cite{de-marneffe-etal-2021-universal} and was produced in the INCEpTION platform~\cite{klie-etal-2018-inception}.
Six annotators (four Uzbek linguists and two NLP engineers) independently double-annotated every sentence after a calibration phase on shared pilot data.
Inter-annotator agreement is high (Table~\ref{tab:inter-annotator}): $>$95\% on lemmas, ${\approx}$95\% on UPOS, and $>$90\% on morphological features.
All conflicts were resolved through adjudication meetings with majority vote or consensus referral to UD guidelines and Uzbek reference grammars.

\textit{UD\_Uzbek-UT (UT).}
Created by \cite{akhundjanova-talamo-2025-universal}, UT comprises 500~sentences (${\approx}$5{,}850 tokens) from two genres: 250 \emph{news} and 250 \emph{fiction}.
Annotation was semi-automatic (automatic tokenization and lemmatization) with full manual correction of POS tags and dependency relations.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We build on Stanza~\cite{qi-etal-2020-stanza}, extending its POS tagger and dependency parser with BERT support.
The pipeline (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}) proceeds in four stages:
(i)~\textbf{encoding} (FastText or TahrirchiBERT),
(ii)~\textbf{subword-to-token fusion} (BERT only),
(iii)~\textbf{morphosyntactic tagging} (UPOS/XPOS/UFeats),
(iv)~\textbf{dependency parsing} (arcs + labels).
Training is \emph{sequential}: the tagger is trained first, its predictions replace gold POS in the data, and the parser is then trained on the re-tagged output.

  \label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure}

% ----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Token Representations}
\label{sec:method_repr}

We compare two representation regimes:
\textbf{(a)~FastText} (static, 300\,d): pre-trained Uzbek vectors (\texttt{cc.uz.300.vec})~\cite{bojanowski-etal-2017-enriching}; and
\textbf{(b)~TahrirchiBERT} (contextual, 768\,d): a monolingual Uzbek BERT (\texttt{tahrirchi-bert-base})~\cite{Mamasaidov2023TahrirchiBERT}, chosen over BERTbek (news-centric) and mBERT (Uzbek-underrepresented) for its broad-domain pretraining that matches the heterogeneous treebank genres.
The encoder is \emph{frozen}; we extract last-layer hidden states and apply a fusion function (\S\ref{sec:method_fusion}) to obtain one 768-d vector per UD token.

The final token vector concatenates four channels:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}_i =
  \big[\,
    \mathbf{e}_{\text{word}}(w_i);\;
    \mathbf{e}_{\text{char}}(w_i);\;
    \mathbf{e}_{\text{upos}}(\hat{p}_i);\;
    \mathbf{e}_{\text{ctx}}(w_i)
  \,\big],
  \label{eq:tokenvec}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_{\text{word}}$ is a pretrained word embedding, $\mathbf{e}_{\text{char}}$ a character-LSTM representation, $\mathbf{e}_{\text{upos}}(\hat{p}_i)$ a 50-d embedding of the tagger's \emph{predicted} UPOS label, and $\mathbf{e}_{\text{ctx}}$ the fused BERT vector (absent in the FastText-only configuration).

% ----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Subword-to-Token Fusion}
\label{sec:method_fusion}

BERT WordPiece boundaries rarely coincide with UD tokens.
For a UD token $t$ split into $k$ subwords with hidden states $\{\mathbf{h}_1,\dots,\mathbf{h}_k\}$, we compare two aggregations:

\paragraph{Last-subword fusion (linguistically motivated).}
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{e}_{\text{ctx}}(t) = \mathbf{h}_k .
  \label{eq:lastsub}
\end{equation}
Uzbek is predominantly suffixing: the rightmost subword typically encodes the outermost grammatical morpheme---the very signal that UD tagging and attachment rely on.
For instance, \textit{bolalarning} (``of the children'') is tokenized as \texttt{bola\;\#\#lar\;\#\#ning}; the last subword \texttt{\#\#ning} carries the genitive suffix.\footnote{A second example: \textit{ko'rganmisiz} (``have you seen?'') $\to$ \texttt{ko'r\;\#\#gan\;\#\#mi\;\#\#siz}; the final \texttt{\#\#siz} carries 2\textsc{pl} agreement.}

\paragraph{Mean pooling (language-agnostic baseline).}
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{e}_{\text{ctx}}(t) = \tfrac{1}{k}\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{h}_i .
  \label{eq:meanpool}
\end{equation}
This makes no typological assumption, serving as a controlled comparator under the same encoder.

% ----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Tagger and Parser}
\label{sec:method_models}

\textit{Joint morphosyntactic tagger.}
A BiLSTM contextualizes the token sequence; task-specific softmax heads jointly predict UPOS, XPOS, and UFeats.
To avoid oracle leakage, the parser receives only \emph{predicted} UPOS embeddings $\mathbf{e}_{\text{upos}}(\hat{p}_i)$.

\textit{Deep biaffine dependency parser.}
Following Dozat and Manning~\cite{DBLP:conf/iclr/DozatM17}, BiLSTM states are projected into head/dependent subspaces via MLPs, and biaffine scoring produces arc and relation scores. The output is a labeled dependency tree over UD tokens.

\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

Our experiments address three controlled comparisons:
(i)~\emph{embedding type}---static FastText vectors versus contextual TahrirchiBERT representations, both held constant with respect to fusion and data;
(ii)~\emph{subword-to-token fusion}---linguistically-motivated last-subword selection versus language-agnostic mean pooling, both using the same TahrirchiBERT encoder;
and (iii)~\emph{data setting}---training on UzUDT alone (451~sentences) versus the merged UzUDT+UT corpus (781~sentences).

\textit{Run matrix.}
We adopt a structured multi-factor design with $3$~embedding/fusion configurations $\times$ $2$~data settings, yielding \textbf{6~experimental runs} (Table~\ref{tab:matrix}).
\textbf{E1} uses static FastText embeddings (\texttt{cc.uz.300}, 300-dim) without subword fusion.
\textbf{E2} uses TahrirchiBERT with \emph{last-subword} fusion, selecting the hidden state of the final WordPiece subword per UD token to preserve suffix-level morphosyntactic cues.
\textbf{E3} uses TahrirchiBERT with \emph{mean pooling}, averaging all subword hidden states per UD token.
Within each configuration, the \texttt{.1}~suffix denotes training on UzUDT only and the \texttt{.2}~suffix denotes training on the merged UzUDT+UT corpus.

\subsection{Training Protocol and Evaluation}
\label{sec:training_eval}

\textit{Pipeline training.}
For each of the six runs, we first train a BiLSTM-based morphosyntactic tagger (UPOS, XPOS, UFeats) and use it to re-tag the training, development, and test files with predicted POS labels.
A deep biaffine dependency parser~\cite{DBLP:conf/iclr/DozatM17} is then trained on the re-tagged data so that it never sees gold POS tags, matching the realistic inference setting.
Test-set evaluation is performed with the standard CoNLL~2018 shared-task script.

\textit{Hyperparameters and model selection.}
All models are trained within a modified Stanza~\cite{qi-etal-2020-stanza} fork using the Adam optimizer ($\beta=(0.9,0.999)$, $\text{lr}=3\!\times\!10^{-3}$, batch\,=\,5{,}000 tokens, dropout\,=\,0.33).
Training runs for a maximum of 50{,}000 steps with early stopping on dev-set performance (AllTags for the tagger, LAS for the parser).
All remaining architecture and optimization settings follow Stanza defaults; the parser uses a single-layer MLP (dim\,=\,500) for both arc and relation scoring, and UPOS embeddings of dimension~50 are concatenated into the parser input.
In TahrirchiBERT configurations, the BERT encoder is \emph{not fine-tuned}; we extract frozen hidden states and apply the fusion function to produce 768-dim token vectors.

\textit{Metrics.}
For tagging we report UPOS, XPOS, and UFeats accuracy.
For parsing we report UAS and LAS.
All scores are computed on the held-out test split using the UD evaluation script (\texttt{conll18\_ud\_eval.py}).

\subsection{Observations}
\label{sec:observations}

\textit{Observation~1: Contextual vs.\ static representations.}
TahrirchiBERT consistently outperforms FastText for UPOS tagging, with gains of +3.26 on UzUDT alone (E2.1\,vs.\,E1.1) and +4.82 on merged data (E2.2\,vs.\,E1.2).
The improvement is amplified by corpus size: UFeats flips from $-$1.24 on small data to +4.11 on merged data, suggesting a minimum data threshold for BERT to learn fine-grained morphological feature bundles.
LAS gains are more modest (+1.41 to +2.95), indicating that contextual embeddings have a larger impact on local tagging decisions than on structural dependency predictions under severe data scarcity.
The per-run evaluation trajectories in Figure~\ref{fig:app:tagger_dynamics} (Appendix) corroborate this pattern: BERT runs (E2/E3) consistently track above FastText (E1) on UPOS and UFeats throughout training, with clear separation visible from early steps.

\textit{Observation~2: Subword-to-token fusion.}
The comparison between last-subword (E2) and mean pooling (E3) reveals a task-dependent pattern.
For \emph{dependency parsing}, last-subword fusion is clearly superior: the LAS advantage is +2.64 on UzUDT and widens to \textbf{+3.76} on merged data, confirming that suffix-positional cues become \emph{more} exploitable as training data grows.
This gap is consequential: mean pooling with BERT (E3.2, LAS\,=\,60.05) actually falls behind static FastText on merged data (E1.2, LAS\,=\,62.40), while last-subword BERT (E2.2, LAS\,=\,63.81) surpasses both---demonstrating that the correct fusion strategy is essential for BERT to outperform FastText on parsing.
For POS tagging the picture is mixed: XPOS consistently favors mean pooling ($-$2.35 on merged data), likely because language-specific tags are determined by the full word form rather than the final suffix, whereas UPOS and UFeats show a weak but consistent last-subword advantage on merged data (+1.06 and +0.70, respectively).
The dev-score and LAS/UAS curves in Figure~\ref{fig:app:parser_dynamics} (Appendix) visually confirm the fusion gap: last-subword runs plateau at higher parsing metrics than their mean-pooling counterparts across both data settings.

\textit{Observation~3: Cross-treebank augmentation.}
Merging UzUDT with UD\_Uzbek-UT produces the single largest accuracy improvement in the study.
LAS jumps by +11.16 for FastText (E1.1$\to$E1.2) and +9.62 for TahrirchiBERT (E2.1$\to$E2.2)---far exceeding the BERT upgrade effect.
The interaction between BERT and data is synergistic, not merely additive: BERT's UFeats advantage swings from $-$1.24 (small data) to +4.11 (merged), a +5.35 amplification.
The two treebanks are structurally complementary in genre (literature/academic vs.\ news/fiction), UPOS distribution (PROPN 0.3\% in UzUDT vs.\ 5.2\% in UT), and dependency-relation coverage (\texttt{advcl} is 4$\times$ more frequent in UzUDT; \texttt{compound:lvc} is 10$\times$ more frequent in UT), explaining why even a modest increase from 451 to 781 training sentences yields such large gains.
The data effect is equally strong under mean pooling: E3.2 LAS improves by +8.50 over E3.1, confirming that the augmentation benefit is robust across fusion strategies.

\textit{Observation~4: Training dynamics.}
All twelve models converge rapidly, reaching their best dev-set checkpoint within 5--18\% of the 50{,}000-step budget (typically 300--1{,}300 steps).
BERT configurations converge slightly earlier than FastText (steps~600--1{,}000 vs.\ 800--1{,}300), suggesting that contextual representations provide a stronger initialization.
Total wall-clock training time for all twelve models is approximately 265~minutes on a single NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU.
Full training curves including loss, dev scores, learning-rate schedules, and GPU memory allocation are provided in Figures~\ref{fig:app:parser_dynamics} and~\ref{fig:app:tagger_dynamics} (Appendix).

\textit{Observation~5: Annotation heterogeneity in merged data.}
Merging two independently developed treebanks inevitably introduces annotation inconsistencies.
To quantify this effect, we compared label distributions in the intersecting deprel inventory.
The UT treebank fails UD validation with 92~warnings, predominantly \texttt{obl}~tokens that should be \texttt{nmod} according to UD~v2 guidelines.
These misanalyses inflate \texttt{obl} frequency by ${\approx}$14\% in the merged training set relative to a corrected distribution.
Despite this noise, the merged-data setting consistently outperforms the UzUDT-only setting across all six configuration pairs, suggesting that the coverage benefits of genre complementarity outweigh the annotation noise.

\subsection{Error Analysis}
\label{sec:error_analysis}

To characterise remaining parsing challenges, we manually analysed errors in 100 randomly sampled test sentences from our best system (E2.2: TahrirchiBERT + last-subword + merged data). Table~\ref{tab:error_analysis} summarises the distribution of these errors by their linguistic source.
Three patterns dominate, suggesting clear avenues for targeted improvements. First, \emph{PP/oblique attachment} (28\%) is driven by \texttt{obl}/\texttt{nmod} ambiguity and exacerbated by the UT inconsistencies from Observation~5, highlighting a need for annotation harmonisation. Second, \emph{coordination scope} errors (22\%) arise in multi-clause conjuncts—a known limitation for biaffine parsers lacking global constraints—suggesting higher-order parsing could be beneficial. Finally, \emph{subordinate clause attachment} errors (18\%) reflect the difficulty of capturing Uzbek's flexible clause ordering.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \textsc{UzUDT}, a 684-sentence gold-standard Uzbek UD
treebank, and a controlled study of two lightweight robustness
strategies for low-resource parsing: linguistically-motivated
last-subword fusion and cross-treebank data augmentation.
Among three embedding/fusion configurations and two data settings,
cross-treebank training is the dominant factor (LAS
+9.62--11.16), while last-subword fusion is critical specifically
for dependency parsing (LAS +3.76 over mean pooling on merged data).
The best system (TahrirchiBERT, last-subword, merged) achieves
85.08~UPOS, 72.39~UAS, and 63.81~LAS.

\textit{Data \& Models Availability}.
Both treebanks are released through the UD website.
The merged data in experiments and full research logs are included in the GitHub\footnote{\url{https://github.com/SanatbekMatlatipov/robust-parsing-uzbek}}. The full twelve trained model checkpoints are available at
\footnote{\url{https://huggingface.co/Sanatbek/uzudt}}.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
Our findings should be interpreted in light of two constraints: the small development sets (45 and 78 sentences), which increase sensitivity during early stopping, and the sequential UT split, which creates a marked genre mismatch between news-heavy training data and a fiction-heavy test set.
In addition, UD\_Uzbek-UT contains validation inconsistencies and shallower morphological annotation than UzUDT (notably for possessor agreement and evidentiality), introducing some label noise into merged-corpus experiments.
